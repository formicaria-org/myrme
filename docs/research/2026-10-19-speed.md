# Making Myrme faster (research and measurements, 2026-10-19)

> **Status: second session done on 2026-10-19; nothing adopted yet.** Everything below was measured
> or verified unless it says otherwise, and each number names its run. A measure is adopted by a
> dated entry in `docs/context/decisions.md`; §4 lists the decisions waiting for the owner, §5 what
> is left to measure.

**The owner:** *"research methods that can make myrme faster: for instance dedicated runtimes for
edge devices? Other measures?"*, then *"it is really slow"* on the phone, *"do a thorough search and
benchmark"*, *"consider also the custom kernels like the one from Arm"*. Second session: *"go on with
everything that does not require the phone"*, then *"in parallel do the phone remaining part"*, with
*"be careful and keep these tests safe … regarding my data and other phone functionalities"*.

**How.**
- **First session:** four research agents (runtimes and backends; model-side measures; system-level
  measures in our stack; the Dimensity 7300 class specifically), about 550k tokens in all, each
  claim with its source. Then measurements with `pixi run eval-speed`: the method evals, with
  llama-server's own timings per request.
- **Second session:** no agents. The resume plan of the first session (its §5), run in sequence:
  accuracy on the laptop GPU, speed on the phone with `llama-bench` over USB, plus a prompt-cache
  probe and a LiteRT-LM build.
- **Phone safety:** every phone run used only binaries in `/data/local/tmp/myrme-bench`, read only
  `dumpsys battery`, wrote nothing outside that folder, and stopped no process except our own
  `llama-bench` by its exact PID. Before every configuration a guard waited for the battery to be
  below 38 °C and stopped the sweep above 45 °C or under 25 % charge; no guard fired (25–33 °C
  throughout, 57–59 % on USB power).

**Hardware.**
- **Phone:** Xiaomi 24095PCADG, MediaTek Dimensity 7300 (MT6878): 4× Cortex-A78 + 4× A55, Armv8.2
  with dotprod, no i8mm or SME; Mali-G615 MC2; Android 16. Runtime b10081 + llguidance.
- **Laptop:** Intel i7-11800H (8 cores, AVX-512), RTX 3050 Laptop 4 GB. Runtime b10076 + llguidance
  (the Vulkan build formicaria ships, `formicaria/agents/runtime`).

**Where the raw data is.** `~/bench-scratch` on the laptop (not in the repo): `sweeps.md` (first
phone sweep), `sweeps2.md`–`sweeps6.md` with `.raw` logs and `phone-qwen.md` (second session),
`run-*.log` (accuracy), `layout-*.log`, `layoutB-*.log`, `layout-ab.md` and `cache-probe-B.md`
(prompt layout), `typed-tiles.md`, `invalid/` (runs found invalid, kept for the record),
`keep/scripts/` (every script used, including `cache_probe.py`), `keep/prompt-layout-2026-10-19.patch`
and `keep/prompt-layout-B-2026-10-19.patch` (measure 3 as code, not applied to `main`) and
`keep/candidate-models.sha256` (the candidate GGUFs, deleted after the study, to re-fetch exactly).

## 1. Where the time goes (measured, first session)

### Phone, lfm2.5-vl-450m Q8_0, 4 threads, ctx 4096 (the shipped phone setup)

| method | requests | prompt tok | prefill tok/s | prefill ms | gen tok | decode tok/s | s/request |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| meetings | 34 | 77 | 306 | 253 | 93 | 23 | 4.3 |
| research | 8 | 110 | 360 | 306 | 137 | 24 | 6.0 |
| edits | 14 | 111 | 367 | 302 | 16 | 26 | 0.9 |
| recipe | 60 | 141 | 389 | 361 | 315 | 23 | 14.0 |
| image (handwriting) | 30 | 1926 | 102 | 18798 | 441 | 21 | 38.8 |

- **Text: reading is fast, writing is the cost.** First token in 0.25–0.36 s; the answer comes at
  23–26 tok/s, so a recipe's ~315 tokens take ~13 s.
- **Photos: the vision encoder is the cost.** A 4128×2322 page becomes 9 tiles; each takes ~1.55 s to
  encode, so ~14 s of the ~19 s "prefill" is encoding (measured with `llama-mtmd-cli`, §2.1).
- `llama-bench`: pp512 466, pp2048 417, tg64 37 tok/s. The CPU variant loaded is
  `libggml-cpu-android_armv8.2_2.so` (dotprod; repacked kernels active).
- Threads: 2 → 250, **4 → 448**, 6 → 359, 8 → 364 tok/s prefill. The shipped `threads_mobile = 4`
  is right.
- The eval cases are short (≈100 prompt tokens). Real notes are 1–6k tokens; see `llama-bench` rows.

## 2. Experiments

### 2.1 Page photos: tiles and projector (phone and laptop)

One real page (4128×2322), `llama-mtmd-cli` on the phone, 4 threads:

| page size | projector | image tiles | encode per tile | encode total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| original | F16 | 9 | 1.55 s | ~14 s |
| 1024 px long side | F16 | 1 | 1.53 s | **1.5 s** |
| original | Q8_0 | 9 | 1.84 s | ~16.5 s |
| 1024 px long side | Q8_0 | 1 | 1.82 s | 1.8 s |

- **Fewer tiles cut encoding ~9×** — but see accuracy below: it is not usable with this model.
- **The Q8_0 projector is ~18% slower than F16 on this CPU**, which has FP16 arithmetic. Keep F16.

**Handwriting** (30 pages, on the phone):

| page size | prompt tokens | prompt time | answer tokens | answer time | mean WER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original (4128 px) | 1926 | 18.4 s | 429 | 19.2 s | 96.3% |
| 1536 px | 1926 (same tiles) | 19.2 s | 573 | 25.9 s | 82.7% |
| 1024 px (1 tile) | **374** | **3.3 s** | **940** | 38.9 s | 96.2% |

- The phone model cannot read handwriting at any size, so handwriting cannot decide the tile
  question. With one tile it loops: 18 of 30 answers run past 900 tokens.
- **DRY repetition control (multiplier 0.8) is not the fix:** answers shorten (mean 576 tokens) but
  turn into varied nonsense, WER 254.6% (laptop GPU, 1024 px).

**Typed pages decide it** (30 Wikibooks recipe texts, CC BY-SA, rendered as photo-sized pages;
`~/bench-scratch/typed-cases.json`; lfm2.5-vl-450m Q8_0 + F16 projector, laptop GPU, b10076):

| page size | mean WER | eval wall time |
|---|---|---|
| original (9 tiles) | **49.0%** | 190 s |
| 1024 px (1 tile) | **101.0%** | 269 s |

- **One tile doubles the errors and is slower overall**, because the answers run long. Measure 4 of
  the first session (fewer tiles) is **rejected for the 450M**. It could be asked again of a stronger
  reader.

### 2.2 Quant formats and kernels (phone, `llama-bench -t 4`, tok/s)

Second session, the three builds run one after another per model so heat cannot favour one build
(`sweeps2.md`): **release** (b10081, the variant chosen at run time), **flags** (the control: built
with `-march=armv8.2-a+dotprod+fp16`, KleidiAI off) and **KleidiAI** (same flags, KleidiAI on).

| model | build | read 512 | read 2048 | write |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 450M **Q8_0** (shipped) | release | 471 | 393 | 36 |
| | flags (control) | 457 | 411 | 38 |
| | **KleidiAI** | **694** | **600** | 40 |
| 450M **Q4_0** | release | 503 | 446 | **65** |
| | flags (control) | 492 | 439 | 64 |
| | KleidiAI | 471 | 430 | 56 ± 9 |
| 450M **Q4_K_M** | release | 397 | 356 | **59** |
| | flags (control) | 394 | 360 | 61 |
| | KleidiAI | 398 | 362 | 60 |
| 1.2B Q4_0 (first session) | release | 147 | 141 | 20 |
| 1.2B Q4_K_M (first session) | release | 116 | 105 | 19 |
| **Qwen3.5-0.8B Q4_0** | release | 163 | 153 | 22 |
| | KleidiAI | 166 | 147 | 22 |
| Qwen3.5-0.8B Q8_0 | release | 171 | 149 | 14 |
| | KleidiAI | 222 | 196 | 15 |

- **4-bit weights write ~65% faster than Q8_0** (59–65 vs 36 tok/s). Text methods spend most of
  their time writing (§1), so this is still the largest text speed-up measured.
- **The KleidiAI gain on Q8_0 is KleidiAI's, not the compiler flags':** the control reads like the
  release (457/411 vs 471/393); KleidiAI reads +47% at 512 and +53% at 2048 tokens.
- **Why only Q8_0.** Read from the KleidiAI build's own `libggml-cpu.so`:
  - KleidiAI in this llama.cpp has entry points for two formats only,
    `ggml_kleidiai_select_kernels_q4_0` and `…_q8_0`. **Q4_K never reaches KleidiAI**, so all three
    builds run the same llama.cpp kernels on it (equal within noise).
  - For Q4_0 on a dotprod-only CPU, KleidiAI's kernels (`qsi8d32p…qsi4c32p…neon_dotprod`) do the
    same arithmetic as llama.cpp's own repacked Q4_0 kernels (blocks of 32, int8 dot products, one
    scale per block): a tie. Its large published gains need i8mm or SME2, which the A78 lacks.
  - For Q8_0 it uses `qai8dxp…qsi8cxp…`: weights **re-quantized at load to one scale per output
    channel** instead of one per 32 weights. That skips per-block scale work — the source of the
    gain — and **changes the numbers the model computes**. Whether answers change is **not
    measured** (§5).
- **Qwen3.5-0.8B runs at about the 1.2B's speed** (153 read, 22 write on Q4_0): ~2.9× slower
  reading and ~3× slower writing than the 450M Q4_0. It is a dense model; LFM2.5 is a hybrid with
  cheaper layers.

**The methods themselves on the phone** (`pixi run eval-speed` over USB, release b10081, 4 threads,
ctx 4096 as shipped; Qwen3.5-0.8B Q4_0 with thinking off, `phone-qwen.md`; the 450M row is §1):

| method | requests | Qwen prompt tok / prefill tok/s | Qwen gen tok / decode tok/s | **Qwen s/request** | **450M Q8_0 s/request** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| meetings | 34 | 88 / 101 | 37 / 13 | **3.7** | 4.3 |
| research | 8 | 114 / 108 | 80 / 14 | **6.9** | 6.0 |
| edits | 14 | 115 / 111 | 23 / 13 | **2.8** | 0.9 |
| recipe | 60 | 134 / 111 | 405 / 14 | **30.9** | 14.0 |

- **Inside the methods Qwen reads at ~105 and writes at ~13–14 tok/s**, below `llama-bench`'s
  153 / 22: short prompts, the grammar, and the phone warming to 37 °C over 40 minutes.
- **Per request it costs what its answers cost.** Meetings is *faster* than the shipped model
  (37-token answers instead of 93); research is about equal; edits, which is mostly reading, is
  3× slower; recipes are 2.2× slower (longer answers at a lower rate).
- Against what a person gets: meetings 13/17 instead of 5/17, edits 3/7 instead of 0/7, recipes
  245 of 257 ingredients instead of 102.

**Photos on the phone: Qwen3.5-0.8B does not fit as configured.**
- **At the shipped 4096-token window it cannot read a full page at all:** its projector turns a
  handwriting page into ~4,200 tokens (the 450M's: 1,926), and the server refuses the request
  ("exceeds the available context size").
- **At 8192, each page takes 71–200 s** on the phone, measured on the server (3 pages; 2,243–4,203
  prompt tokens; `phone-qwen-image-server.log`). The 450M takes 38.8 s.
- **That is past the client's 120 s request timeout** (`OpenAiStep`, `openai.rs`), which production
  uses too: every page was cancelled by the client before its answer, so no accuracy was recorded
  on the phone. Most full-size pages would fail in production the same way.
- Ways round it, none measured: a lower `--image-max-tokens` (but the server warns Qwen-VL needs
  ≥1024 image tokens, and §2.1 showed fewer tiles hurt the 450M); a longer timeout for reading;
  or keeping the 450M (or a dedicated reader) for photos while Qwen does the text methods.
- **Measured afterwards: capped at 1024 image tokens it fits and reads better.**

  | Qwen3.5-0.8B pages | uncapped | **capped at 1024** |
  |---|---|---|
  | typed, 30 (laptop) | 7.0% WER | **2.3%** |
  | handwriting, 30 (laptop) | 49.3% | **43.4%** |
  | handwriting, 10 (phone, ctx 4096, 120 s timeout) | did not fit | **44.8%**, median **28 s** a page |

  On the phone 8 of 10 pages took 22–43 s (the 450M: 38.8 s). Two pages looped (~2,100–2,400
  tokens, 113–122 s); one was cancelled at the 120 s limit. `capcheck-*.log` in `~/bench-scratch`.

### 2.3 Accuracy of the candidates (laptop RTX 3050, b10076)

Accuracy does not depend on the hardware, so it was measured on the laptop. One run per row; the
meetings and recipe columns are the **constrained** answers, as production runs them.

| model | meetings exact | research exact / grounded | edits exact | recipe exact / ingredients found (of 257) / invented |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 450M Q8_0 (shipped) | 5/17 | 5/8 / 4/8 | 0/7 | 8/30 / 102 / 18 |
| 450M Q4_0 | 5/17 | 5/8 / 4/8 | 0/7 | 7/30 / **59** / 18 |
| 450M **Q4_K_M** | 5/17 | 6/8 / 4/8 | 0/7 | 9/30 / 89 / 16 |
| 1.2B Q4_0 | **13/17** | 5/8 / 7/8 | **3/7** | 3/30 / 157 / 25 |
| 1.2B Q4_K_M | **13/17** | **7/8** / 6/8 | **3/7** | 6/30 / 218 / 12 |
| **Qwen3.5-0.8B Q4_0** (thinking off) | **13/17** | 4/8 / **8/8** | **3/7** | **15/30 / 245 / 2** |
| Qwen3.5-0.8B Q8_0 (thinking off) | 6/17 ⚠ | **7/8 / 8/8** | 2/7 | **16/30** / 234 / 3 |

- **Qwen3.5-0.8B Q4_0 is the most accurate small model measured**: it matches the 1.2B on
  meetings and edits, grounds every research answer, and reads recipes far better than any LFM2.5
  (245 of 257 ingredients, 2 invented). Its free (unconstrained) recipe answers score 16/30, 237,
  1 invented.
- ⚠ **Qwen3.5-0.8B Q8_0 answers `[]` to every meeting.** Checked by hand on `en-simple` at
  temperature 0: Q4_0 returns the meeting, Q8_0 returns `[]`. The file is not damaged (its other
  evals score well); it is a behaviour of that quant on this prompt. Not investigated further.
- **Qwen3.5 thinks by default.** With its template's default, every answer spends the whole token
  budget on reasoning and returns empty content (a first run scored 0/17 well-formed, ~10× slower;
  moved to `invalid/`). `--chat-template-kwargs {"enable_thinking":false}` turns it off on b10076;
  `--reasoning-budget 0` does not. A catalogue entry for it would need that flag.
- **Qwen3.5-0.8B reads pages far better** (Q4_0 + F16 projector, thinking off, laptop GPU,
  full-size pages; `run-qwen-image-*.log`):

  | pages (30 each) | Qwen3.5-0.8B WER | 450M Q8_0 WER |
  |---|---|---|
  | handwriting | **49.3%** | 96.3% (phone, §2.1) |
  | typed (Wikibooks recipes) | **7.0%** | 49.0% |

  One typed page failed (97.5%); every other one is at 17.5% or below. The projector in
  `cand/` from the first session was an empty file; unsloth's `mmproj-F16.gguf` (SHA-256 checked)
  was used.
- **Granite-4.0-1B: dropped, not measured** (owner, 2026-10-19: *"not really useful"*, with
  Qwen3.5-0.8B already covering an Apache-2.0 option). Two facts found on the way:
  - the first download was truncated (171 MB of 975 MB; "corrupted or incomplete");
  - on the laptop's **Vulkan GPU path in b10076 it outputs garbage** (`__**__$__…`), while the same
    file on the CPU answers sensibly. The garbage runs are in `invalid/`.

### 2.4 The Mali GPU (Vulkan, llama.cpp)

- The GPU reports subgroup size 16, 32 KB shared memory, **no integer dot product and no matrix
  cores**.
- **Upstream Vulkan computes wrong results on it:** `test-backend-ops` failed 25 of 982 matmul cases
  (the known subgroup-16 bug, llama.cpp #28637). **The fix from PR #30106 (unmerged), ported to
  b10081, passes 982/982** and ~11,000 further cases (`keep/builds/mali-subgroup16-fix-pr30106-port.patch`).
- **Speed, all layers on the GPU** (`sweeps3.md`, `-p 512 -n 64 -r 2`, tok/s):

| model | Mali read 512 | Mali write | CPU read 512 (release) | CPU write (release) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 450M Q8_0 | 16.3 | 20.6 | 471 | 36 |
| 450M Q4_0 | 15.5 | 25.1 | 503 | 65 |
| 450M Q4_K_M | 15.7 | 27.2 | 397 | 59 |
| 1.2B Q4_0 | 4.3 | 8.8 | 147 | 20 |

- **The Mali reads ~30× slower than the CPU and writes at about half its speed.** Measure 7 (Mali
  via Vulkan in llama.cpp) is **rejected**: no setting closes a 30× gap.
- The Vulkan build with no layers on the GPU (`-ngl 0`) still sends large batches to the GPU and
  read 14.5 tok/s; that block was stopped and not repeated (§1, *Correction*).
- The planned flash-attention on/off run on the GPU was skipped (owner): it could not change the
  outcome.

### 2.5 Runtime settings on the phone (release build, 4 threads, `sweeps2.md`)

| setting | 450M Q8_0 | 450M Q4_K_M | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| flash attention off → on (read 2048 / write) | 396 → 413 / 31 → 34 | 329 → 356 / 50 → 59 | **on** is faster; the shipped `auto` should pick it (to confirm in the server log) |
| `-ub` 128 / 256 / 512 / 1024 (read 2048) | 380 / 396 / 404 / 409 | 357 / 360 / 360 / 358 | keep the default 512 |

### 2.6 LiteRT-LM (Google) on the phone

**Setup.** LiteRT-LM at commit `9a5f0ae` built for Android arm64 with Bazel 7.6.1
(`keep/scripts/build.sh`; 15 min, 4,379 actions). The model is the 8-bit `Qwen3-0.6B.litertlm`
(614 MB), so llama.cpp ran the same model as **Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0** (Qwen's official GGUF, 639 MB). The
GPU path is LiteRT's OpenCL accelerator (`libLiteRtClGlAccelerator.so`, the ML Drift engine): all
1,306 decode operations went to it. On the CPU, XNNPACK took 1,129 of 1,306, in 228 pieces.

**Speed** (`sweeps5.md`, `sweeps6.md`; LiteRT with a ~660-token prompt, the warm second of two runs;
llama.cpp `llama-bench -p 512 -n 64 -r 3`; tok/s):

| runtime | read | write | first token |
|---|---|---|---|
| llama.cpp release, CPU 4 threads | **246** | **22.9** | — |
| llama.cpp KleidiAI, CPU 4 threads | **344** | **23.7** | — |
| LiteRT-LM, CPU 4 threads | 46 | 2.9 | 14.6 s |
| LiteRT-LM, Mali GPU | 92 | 5.2 | 7.4 s (plus 21.6 s to start the GPU) |

- **LiteRT-LM is slower on every count:** on the CPU ~5× slower at reading and ~8× slower at
  writing than llama.cpp; on the Mali ~2.7× slower at reading and ~4.4× slower at writing than
  llama.cpp on the CPU. Its GPU is faster than llama.cpp's Vulkan on the same Mali (§2.4), but
  still far below our CPU.
- **Rejected for this phone.** The published 500–800 tok/s were for a 7-core Mali-G715; this
  Mali-G615 MC2 has two cores and no integer dot product.
- **Caveats, honestly:**
  - `litert_lm_main` ignores `--benchmark_prefill_tokens`/`--benchmark_decode_tokens` (only
    `litert_lm_advanced_main` reads them) and `--max_output_tokens` (it wrote 236–377 tokens). So
    the prompt came from a file, and lengths differ from llama.cpp's fixed 512/64.
  - The `.litertlm` file came from the first session; which conversion settings it used is not
    recorded. A model converted for this exact chip could do better. Nothing published suggests 5–8×.
  - A first run with the built-in short prompt (17 + 70 tokens) gave the same writing speeds (2.2 CPU,
    5.5 GPU).

### 2.7 Prompt layout for the cache (measure 3 in §4)

**The first variant** (`keep/prompt-layout-2026-10-19.patch`, built and tested in a worktree, **not
applied** to `main`):
- **Mail:** the meetings call and the title call send the same short system prompt
  (`prompts::MAIL_SYSTEM`) with the emails first and their own question last (`meetings::ask`), so
  the second call reuses the first one's cached emails.
- **Edits:** `assemble_turn_context` puts the notes before the conversation, so the note stays a
  cached prefix while the conversation grows.
- The latest message is still sent twice (in the history and as the task): ~20 tokens, and the
  2026-10-18 decision ties that history line to eval fidelity; left alone.
- `pixi run test` passes with the change (all suites).

**Cache reuse** (`keep/scripts/cache_probe.py`: llama-server's own `timings.prompt_n`/`cache_n`;
lfm2.5-vl-450m Q8_0 with its projector loaded, laptop CPU; synthetic threads built from the typed
recipe texts, since the eval emails are only ~135 characters):

| pair | thread size | layout | 2nd call: tokens read / cached | 2nd call prefill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| meetings → title | ~1000 tokens | old | 1521 / 0 | 7.0 s |
| | | **new** | **389** / 1255 | **1.2 s** |
| meetings → title | ~4000 tokens | old | 4041 / 1005 | 22.9 s |
| | | **new** | **389** / 4991 | **1.4 s** |
| edits turn 1 → turn 2 | 1200-char note | old | 398 / 236 | 2.2 s |
| | | **new** | **240** / 236 | 1.2 s |

- **Reuse works on LFM2.5's hybrid layers too** (the first session's open question). The title call
  then reads only its own question. Times are the busy laptop CPU's, not the phone's; on the phone
  (~400 tok/s) the 4000-token case would save ~10 s once per conversation.
- The edits saving is bounded: the note shown is at most 1,200 characters (~300 tokens).

**Accuracy** (laptop GPU, constrained meetings, edits at production settings):

| model | eval | old layout | new layout | runs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lfm2.5-vl-450m Q8_0 | meetings | 5/17 | **6/17** | 2 + 2 |
| | edits | 0/7 | 0/7 | 2 + 2 |
| qwen3-vl-4b Q4_K_M | meetings | **15/17** | 14/17 | 3 + 3 |
| | edits | **7/7** | 6/7 | 3 + 3 |

- **The laptop model loses one case per eval, every run:**
  - `question-accepted` (a time proposed as a question, then accepted): the old layout returns it;
    the new one answers `[]`.
  - `latex`: the new layout's edit loses the "Kinetic energy" heading.
- So this first variant is a trade, not a free win.

**Variant B: the speed without the loss** (`keep/prompt-layout-B-2026-10-19.patch`, built in a
worktree, `pixi run test` and clippy clean, **not applied** to `main`).
- **Why a first idea could not work.** The cache reuses only a prefix identical from the first
  token. Keeping each method's own instruction in its own system prompt makes the two calls differ
  at the start, so nothing is reused (the §5 wording of an earlier draft of this doc was wrong).
- **Mail:** one system prompt for both calls, holding **both** instructions under their names
  (`meetings::system(me)`: *Question "meetings": …*, *Question "title": …*), then the emails, then a
  last line `Question: meetings` or `Question: title` (`meetings::ask`). The instructions stay in
  the system prompt, where qwen3-vl-4b reads them best.
- **Edits: unchanged.** Its saving is at most ~300 tokens a turn (the note is capped at 1,200
  characters), and the reorder is what lost `latex`.
- **Cache reuse** (same probe, `cache-probe-B.md`): the title call reads **516** tokens instead of
  1,521 (~1000-token thread) and 4,041 (~4000-token thread): a 127-token longer tail than the first
  variant, the same order of saving.
- **Accuracy, meetings, constrained** (laptop GPU; `layoutB-*.log`):

  | model | old layout | first variant | **variant B** | runs (B) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | lfm2.5-vl-450m Q8_0 | 5/17 | 6/17 | **7/17** | 2 |
  | qwen3-vl-4b Q4_K_M | 15/17 | 14/17 | **16/17** | 3 |

  - On qwen3-vl-4b, variant B **passes `two-meetings`**, which the old layout fails, and keeps
    `question-accepted`. Its one failure, `question-declined`, fails under the old layout too.
  - Every run gave the same score.
- **On the two phone models it is worse** (shipped code, laptop GPU, 2 runs each, identical):

  | model | old layout | **variant B** |
  |---|---|---|
  | lfm2.5-vl-450m **Q4_K_M** (the new phone default) | 5/17 | **3/17** |
  | Qwen3.5-0.8B Q4_0 (the accurate phone choice) | 13/17 | **8/17** |

  So the layout interacts with the model: the instructions-in-one-system-prompt form helps
  qwen3-vl-4b and the Q8_0 450M, and costs the 4-bit 450M two cases and Qwen3.5-0.8B five.
- **Not shipped** (decision 2026-10-19). The meeting pass runs only on the desktop today, where B
  helps, but B would block turning it on for phones. Studied next on two fixed baselines,
  qwen3-vl-4b and the 450M Q4_K_M, with a **variant C**: the meetings call exactly as today, the
  title call reusing its system prompt and emails and adding its own question last — meetings
  accuracy unchanged by construction, the cache still shared. Judging C needs a title eval.
- *(Earlier wording, kept for the record:)* **Variant B is better than today on both models and faster.** It changes no contract item
  (`meetings::ask`/`system` are new; `prompts::MEETINGS_INSTRUCTION` and `TITLE_INSTRUCTION` are
  unchanged).
- **The title call still has no eval** (design rule 5): the title now reads its instruction beside
  the meetings one, and whether its answers change is **unmeasured**.

## 3. What the research says (first session; corrections marked)

### 3.1 In our own stack

- **Prompt layout defeats the prompt cache.** llama-server reuses only the longest common token
  prefix; every method starts with its own system prompt. Measured and built in §2.7.
- **`--cache-reuse` cannot help:** llama-server switches it off whenever a projector is loaded,
  which is always our case (b10076 source). One report recommended it; the source decides.
- **Flags worth an A/B:** drop `--no-warmup`; `-np 1` (our client is sequential); an explicit
  `-cram` (the 8 GiB default host prompt cache is not counted by our memory admission); `-ub` on the
  laptop GPU; `-ctk/-ctv q8_0` on the 4 GB GPU. Flash attention is already `auto`. *(Phone settings
  measured in §2.5; the laptop flags are still not measured.)*
- **llguidance costs little:** about 50 µs per token; grammars are rebuilt per request but our
  schemas are small.
- **Bug found and fixed:** the phone started its model with the global 2048-token window instead of
  the model's 4096 (Myrme 0.5.1).

### 3.2 Runtimes and kernels for the phone

- **The A78 has dotprod only.** llama.cpp's dotprod repacking (Q4_0, Q4_K, Q6_K, Q8_0) is already in
  b10081 and active. **KleidiAI's** large gains need i8mm or SME2; on this CPU it helps Q8_0 only
  (§2.2).
- **Mali GPU via llama.cpp Vulkan:** measured in §2.4 — 30× slower than the CPU at reading.
- **LiteRT-LM (Google):** the only engine with fast published Mali numbers (ML Drift: 500–800 tok/s
  prefill on a 7-core Mali-G715 for 2–3B models). It does JSON-Schema output (LLGuidance) but has
  Mali bugs, its own model format, and no LFM2/LFM2.5 build. **Correction:** the first session's
  text said it was "built and measured on CPU and GPU in §2.2"; it was not. It was built and
  measured in the second session (§2.6).
- **NPU:** no public path runs a 0.5–1.5B LLM on the 7300's APU. Prebuilt LLM files exist only for
  the Dimensity 9300/9400.
- **MNN, MLC, ExecuTorch:** no Mali numbers worth the switch; MNN reports Mali problems.
- **Do not upgrade llama.cpp blindly:** vision encoding on Android CPU is ~2.7× slower on master than
  on b10516 (#27649, closed without a fix). Measure encode time before any runtime upgrade.

### 3.3 Laptops

- An optional **CUDA** runtime for NVIDIA users; one undated third-party report puts Vulkan prefill
  ~3× below CUDA. The CUDA libraries are under NVIDIA's EULA, so it would be an opt-in download.
  *(Second session: the b10076 Vulkan path also gave garbage for Granite-4.0-1B, §2.3.)*
- On Apple M5 with macOS 26.2 or later, Metal 4's tensor path gives 2–3× prompt processing (user
  report); b10076 has it.
- **A smaller default without a GPU** (§1: 70 tok/s on CPU for the 4B model). Not measured yet.

### 3.4 Models

- **Handwriting:** Qwen3.5-2B (Apache-2.0) reports OCR scores at or above Qwen3-VL-4B, at half the
  parameters (vendor numbers).
- **Speech, Italian:** Qwen3-ASR-0.6B (Apache-2.0, runs in llama.cpp) and Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3
  (CC-BY-4.0) report far lower Italian WER than whisper base or small (vendor numbers).
- **Task-tuned small models:** LoRA-tuned 0.8–2B models reach near parity on narrow extraction
  tasks in the literature. Not for research with citations, where no ≤2B model quoted verbatim in
  our bench.
- **Speculative decoding** works with llguidance (the target verifies each drafted token under the
  grammar), but only speeds up writing. The n-gram modes need no extra model and suit copy-heavy
  edits. Not measured.
- **Licence:** the phone default (LFM2.5, LFM Open License v1.0) limits commercial use to entities
  under $10M annual revenue. It is shown honestly before download. Apache-2.0 alternatives of similar
  size exist; **Qwen3.5-0.8B is now measured** (§2.2, §2.3). The owner's decision.

## Mistakes in these sessions, kept so they are not repeated

- A "CPU" table measured with the Vulkan runtime was not CPU-only (§1, *Correction*); the same
  happened with `-ngl 0` on the phone's Vulkan build (§2.4).
- `pkill -f` / `pgrep -f` with a pattern that also appears in the calling shell matched that shell.
  First session: it killed the shell once, and a wait loop never ended (phone idle ~50 min). Second
  session: twice more, a stop command killed its own shell. **Stop by exact PID after reading
  `/proc/<pid>/cmdline`, and never with a pattern the command itself contains.**
- Large downloads went to `/tmp`, which is a 7.5 GB RAM disk here, and filled it.
- A model was evaluated without checking it was whole (Granite, truncated) or that it answered at all
  (Qwen3.5 in thinking mode). **Before an eval, send one request by hand and read the answer.**
- The eval chain first ran without `LLAMA_SERVER` set and failed in seconds; the A/B script kept only
  summary lines, so the flipped cases had to be re-run with full logs. **Keep per-case logs.**
- The LiteRT-LM build needed a host C++ compiler (none on this machine; a conda `gxx` in a throwaway
  pixi environment under `~/bench-scratch` was used), and its Android accelerator libraries arrived
  as **Git LFS pointer stubs** (132 bytes). The real files were fetched from the pinned LiteRT commit
  and checked against the pointers' SHA-256.
- A status report quoted the KleidiAI Q4_K_M row as the release row (§2.2 now reads every row from
  the raw log). The conclusion did not change.
